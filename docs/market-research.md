# Karachi civic-tech problem and alternatives

## Executive summary

Public sources document municipal service-delivery challenges in Karachi, particularly solid-waste management and drainage/flooding. Official complaint channels also exist for city, waste, and water/sewerage services. The opportunity for SolveKey is therefore not to claim that no reporting services exist, but to test whether a clear, photo- and location-supported entry point across several service categories can make reporting and follow-up easier.

The evidence reviewed here does not establish current citywide incident rates, response times, resolution rates, user satisfaction, or whether existing systems interoperate. SolveKey's AI triage, duplicate detection, map, and status timeline are product capabilities and hypotheses to validate—not proven improvements in municipal outcomes.

## Local problem context

- **Solid waste:** The World Bank's Competitive and Livable City of Karachi project record describes critical solid-waste management challenges and includes assistance to Sindh's Local Government Department and the Sindh Solid Waste Management Board. A 2019 academic article also examines Karachi's urban solid-waste management. These sources document a management concern; they do not establish a current amount of uncollected waste or a citywide service rate.
- **Drainage and flooding:** Two studies published in 2023 and 2024 discuss urban flooding and drainage in Karachi. The 2023 case study focuses on DHA and the August 2020 floods; its findings should not be generalized to every neighborhood. KMC lists main roads and storm-water drains among its functions.
- **Water and sewerage:** The Karachi Water & Sewerage Corporation portal presents complaint management for water supply, sewerage, billing, and illegal connections. Its description of the K-IV project is not a measure of current household service reliability.
- **Streetlights:** KMC describes streetlight installation as a municipal initiative. The sources reviewed do not quantify outage prevalence or establish the coverage of a dedicated streetlight complaint channel.

## SolveKey differentiator hypotheses

1. **One starting point across service categories.** KMC, SSWMB, and KWSC publicly present different service responsibilities and entry points. Test whether a unified intake with understandable routing reduces the effort to choose where to report; do not claim the existing channels are ineffective or unable to interoperate without further evidence.
2. **Photo and location for structured triage.** SolveKey accepts a photo and coordinates, then classifies, estimates severity, checks for nearby duplicates, and routes the report. Test whether those fields help departments identify recurring locations and prioritize work. The sources reviewed do not show that AI triage improves response.
3. **Status visibility after submission.** SolveKey emits live report updates and exposes an agent trace. KWSC already advertises complaint tracking, so tracking alone is not unique. A stronger hypothesis is consistent status visibility across multiple service types, contingent on dependable agency handoffs and status updates.
4. **Map-based hotspot exploration.** Flooding research makes drainage and location patterns relevant to planning. Test the map with residents and departments; the reviewed sources do not validate SolveKey's data or show that a public map changes outcomes.
5. **Participation incentives.** SolveKey awards points and levels for participation. This is a product mechanic to evaluate for useful, non-duplicative contributions; no source in this review demonstrates its effect on reporting quality or sustained engagement.

## Evidence gaps and validation plan

Before making impact or market-size claims, SolveKey should gather:

- Representative neighborhood-level counts for waste accumulation, drainage issues, road defects, water interruptions, and streetlight outages.
- Baselines for the existing channels: complaint volume, acknowledgment time, resolution time, closure rate, coverage, and user satisfaction.
- Interviews with residents and staff at KMC, SSWMB, and KWSC about intake, routing, duplicate handling, and handoffs.
- A pilot measuring report completeness, duplicate rates, department acknowledgment, status-update latency, and verified resolution.
- Evidence that departments can receive, own, and update reports before promising cross-agency accountability.
